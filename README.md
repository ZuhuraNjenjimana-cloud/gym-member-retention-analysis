# Will They Stay or Will They Churn?

## Gym Member Engagement & Retention Analysis

**Independent Data Analytics Case Study**

An independent data analytics project analyzing 50,000 gym membership records to identify factors associated with member engagement and churn.

---

## 📊 Project Overview

After completing the Google Data Analytics Professional Certificate, I wanted to move beyond guided coursework and apply my skills to an independent project.

For this case study, I investigated:

- What factors are associated with member churn?
- How does gym engagement relate to retention?
- Does distance from the gym appear to be associated with churn?
- How do weekly class participation and session length relate to observed churn?
- What insights could a fitness business use to improve member retention?

---

## 🔎 Key Findings

- **50,000** gym members were analyzed.
- **45.12%** of members churned by February.
- Members living **20+ km** from the gym had a **67.63%** churn rate compared with **40.67%** among members living under 5 km away.
- Members attending **0 weekly classes** had a **65.33%** churn rate.
- Members averaging **60+ minute sessions** had a **33.80%** churn rate compared with **59.78%** among members averaging under 30 minutes.
- Weekly class participation showed the strongest explanatory relationship among the primary regression analyses.

Recommendations
Based on the observed patterns, the analysis suggests that fitness businesses could:
Prioritize early member engagement.
Monitor members showing limited participation.
Consider accessibility challenges for members farther from the facility.
Encourage consistent and meaningful gym engagement.
Use behavioral indicators alongside demographic information when identifying members who may need additional engagement.

Limitations
This analysis identifies associations rather than causation. Churn was measured through February, and the dataset does not capture factors such as member satisfaction, scheduling constraints, or individual reasons for cancellation.
Because churn is a binary outcome, the linear regression analyses were treated as exploratory. Logistic regression would be more appropriate for future predictive modeling.
